Closet Prayer / Administrator console

Privacy in the administrator console

Implementation notice. Updated October 4, 2026.

This notice describes the group-management pilot, not the separate personal prayer journal or a church's own data-handling policy.

What the console accesses

After you choose to connect, Google supplies an account identity, name, verified email address, and a short-lived access token. Google Picker lets you choose a spreadsheet. The console checks its file type and Editor capability, reads its tab metadata, and downloads the configured Requests and Inbox columns. The inbox may include private contact information.

On upgraded groups, the console also reads ConsoleSettings and ConsoleCommands and can save administrative changes. The requested Google file permission includes reading and editing files authorized for this app, including files selected in earlier sessions. Google permissions operate at the file level, not at the individual-tab level. Keep the spreadsheet restricted to trusted administrators.

Where the data goes

The console sends authenticated API requests directly from your browser to Google. A save appends a command containing prayer wording to the church's private spreadsheet. The browser then sends only that operation's identifier and the group's public submission credential to its Apps Script endpoint. The script reads the command from the private sheet and applies the change there. Google access tokens are never sent to church-provided URLs. Prayer contents are not sent to Closet Prayer hosting, GitHub, or a central account database.

Commands and their outcomes remain in the church's ConsoleCommands tab, including wording that was later changed, declined, or withdrawn. This operational history has no verified author identity and is not a tamper-proof audit log. Church administrators control retention and any private archives. Signing out, withdrawing a published prayer, or clearing browser state does not delete command history or undo a queued change.

Account details, tokens, and downloaded prayers are held in the console page's memory. The console does not write them to localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, or an offline cache, and it has no analytics or prayer-content logging. Signing out, reloading, or leaving the page clears its active application state. Session expiry also disconnects the console. This is not a guarantee of forensic erasure from a device.

Connection diagnostics keep up to 24 timestamps and fixed progress/error step names in page memory. They can record that the Google picker frame loaded or sent a message, but never inspect or record that message's contents. They do not include Google response bodies, keys, tokens, account details, file names, file IDs, or prayer contents. The console does not transmit this report; you can select and copy it yourself when requesting help. Closing a stalled picker through the recovery prompt preserves the report and current Google connection. Signing out or reloading clears it.

Other services

The embeddable submission form is hosted on closetprayer.com, but sends requests directly to the church's service and private Inbox. It includes a public submission credential, not a member invitation or administrator token. Visitors must provide consent; contacts remain administrator-only unless someone manually copies them into published wording. Approved prayers go to group members at their next sync. Group-only is a sharing restriction, not encryption, and downloaded/exported copies may outlive a withdrawal.

When Google connection is configured, the page loads Google's identity and file-picker libraries. Google handles account login, consent, permissions, and its own cookies. GitHub Pages serves the public website and may process ordinary hosting logs, including network information. Their policies also apply: Google Privacy Policy and GitHub Privacy Statement.

Your choices

Some Brave configurations require a site-only cookie exception for Google's embedded picker to work. Allowing all cookies for the console permits third-party cookies for embedded content on that site; it is not a global browser change. Keep other Shields protections enabled, or use a browser where the connection works without changing your preferred settings. The console neither detects nor changes your cookie settings. See Brave cookie help for the tradeoff and how to remove the exception.

Use Sign out to clear the console's session. This does not sign you out of Google everywhere or revoke consent. To remove the app's permission, visit your Google account's third-party connections. Ask the church owner to change spreadsheet sharing or to correct or delete its prayer records. The console does not hold a separate server copy to delete.

Existing records can remain on screen until the next refresh or session expiry after a permission is revoked elsewhere. Avoid shared devices for sensitive pastoral records, and sign out when finished. Group-only labels restrict supported app sharing, not screenshots or copying by a recipient.

Support

For public, non-sensitive software questions, use the project's issue tracker. Never post prayer text, personal contact details, or access tokens there. For questions about a prayer or a church's practices, contact that church directly.
